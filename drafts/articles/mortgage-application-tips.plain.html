What to prepare before your mortgage application

James Okafor
3 August 2026

A little preparation goes a long way toward a smoother, faster mortgage application.

Lenders look at more than just your income. Here's what to have ready before you apply, straight from our mortgage advisers who review these applications every day. Getting the paperwork right the first time is the single biggest factor in how quickly an application moves, and it's almost entirely within your control.

Proof of income

Three to six months of payslips, or two years of accounts if you're self-employed. If you've recently changed jobs or gone self-employed, lenders may ask for a bit more context, such as a letter from your employer confirming your role and salary, or evidence that your new income is stable and likely to continue.

If you're self-employed

Bring your last two years of SA302 forms or tax year overviews alongside your accountant's certificate, if you have one. This is usually the fastest way to confirm income, and having it ready before you apply can shave days off the whole process compared with tracking it down afterwards.

Self-employed applicants often assume it'll be harder to get approved. Having the right paperwork ready from day one makes the biggest difference.
NatWest mortgage adviser

Bank statements

Recent statements help us understand your spending patterns and regular commitments. We typically ask for the last three months across your main current account, and we're looking for consistent income, manageable existing debt, and no unexplained large withdrawals in the run-up to your application. A few unusual transactions won't necessarily hold things up, but being ready to explain them in advance saves a back-and-forth later.

Details of your deposit

Be ready to show where your deposit is coming from, whether it's savings, a gift, or an inheritance. This is a legal requirement, not just a formality, and lenders take it seriously as part of anti-money-laundering checks.

Gifted deposits

If a family member is gifting some or all of your deposit, they'll usually need to sign a short letter confirming it's a gift and not a loan, along with proof of where their own funds came from. It's a quick step, but skipping it can delay an otherwise straightforward application by a week or more.

A realistic budget

Know your monthly limit before you start viewing properties, it keeps the search focused and avoids disappointment further down the line. Stamp duty, survey fees, solicitor costs and moving expenses can add up to several thousand pounds on top of your deposit, so budget for these early rather than discovering them at the last minute.

The applicants who move fastest are the ones who've already budgeted for the extra costs, not just the deposit.
